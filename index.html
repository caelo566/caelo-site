The problem

Decades of mercury, with no map of where it sits

2,000+ t

of mercury released every year by artisanal and small-scale gold mining

80+

countries where up to 20 million artisanal miners work

100M

people directly or indirectly exposed to mercury from the sector

Much of that mercury stays behind in tailings ponds after mining moves on. In water and wet sediment it can turn into methylmercury, which builds up through the food web and reaches communities that rely on fish.

It doesn’t go away on its own. Studies in French Guiana, Australia and California find amalgamation mercury still in soils and sediments 60 to 150 years after mining ended, and mining ponds in the Peruvian Amazon convert mercury to methylmercury five to seven times faster than rivers.

Remediation money is limited, and nobody can prioritize sites that haven’t been found. Caelo builds that inventory.

Sources: UNEP planetGOLD programme; Guédron et al. 2011, Water Research; Schneider et al., Environmental Pollution; USGS; Gerson et al. 2020, Science Advances

Approach

Three passes over every scene

  1. Pass 1

    Detect

    Multispectral Sentinel-2 imagery is screened for the surface signatures of mine tailings, at 10 m resolution, across whole districts at once.

  2. Pass 2

    Filter

    Sentinel-1 radar, time-series data and terrain (elevation and slope) separate standing ponds from rivers, wet ground and recently disturbed land, so only quiet legacy sites stay on the list.

  3. Pass 3

    Rank

    Candidates are ordered for field characterization and remediation, giving planners a short list instead of a whole map.

How we work

Built for cleanup, not policing

Remediation first

Caelo maps legacy contamination to direct cleanup. It is not a surveillance or enforcement product.

Outputs indicate likely tailings and conditions that favour mercury transport. They never claim measured concentrations.

Industry challenges

How we answer the sector’s pressures

Water and tailings

Legacy ponds are found, reprocessed and reclaimed, with process water reused on site rather than discharged.

Safety

No open burning of amalgam, the largest source of miners’ mercury-vapour exposure. Legacy mercury is captured in a closed retort, and remote screening means fewer field trips into difficult terrain.

Digitalization and AI

Satellite imagery and machine learning replace district-wide field reconnaissance.

Sustainability

Gold-funded cleanup, progressive reclamation to a background-referenced endpoint, and a 5% community commitment.

Community health

Business model · partner company

A cleanup that pays for itself

AuroClear Technologies Inc. reprocesses legacy tailings without mercury. The gold left behind by mercury mining pays to remove the mercury and reclaim the ground, so remediation is funded by what it recovers, not by grants alone. Its first pilot plant is planned for Guyana in 2027.

Pre-optimized feedstock. Caelo selects the ground from orbit, and a proprietary pre-conditioning step prepares clay-rich pond sediment before it reaches proven gravity equipment.

AuroClear is committed to writing a 5% community commitment into its corporate articles.
